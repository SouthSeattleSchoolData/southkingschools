Safety: discipline, violent incidents and weapons

No public dataset counts "shootings" or "bombings" by school. What does exist: federal Civil Rights Data Collection (CRDC) incident counts, OSPI's exclusionary-discipline rates, and news reports. All three are below, each with its limits stated.

Bellevue's near-zero discipline rate (<0.015% in 2024-25, down from 0.65% in 2022-23) is unusual.     5. Get the records yourself

    Washington's Public Records Act (RCW 42.56) lets anyone request district records. The request below asks for the data that isn't published: weapons incidents, threat assessments, lockdowns, bomb threats and harassment/bullying (HIB) reports. Send one to each district's public records officer.

    Student wellbeing: Healthy Youth Survey 2025

    The Healthy Youth Survey (HYS) is an anonymous survey of 6th, 8th, 10th and 12th graders, given every two years. It's the best source on bullying, feeling safe, and suicidal thoughts. But the state releases district and school results only with the superintendent's permission. Results that identify a school or district can be published only with the superintendent's written OK; otherwise data from at least 3 schools and 3 districts must be combined (HYS 2025 Data Analysis Manual, pp. 24–25, 121–122). Seattle Public Schools posts its district report publicly, so Seattle's numbers appear below.

    Youth suicide deaths

    No agency reports student suicide deaths by school or district, and that's deliberate: small counts could identify a child, and publicizing clusters can raise risk for other students. The closest public figures:

    • Washington State: the suicide death rate for ages 10–17 rose from 4.7 per 100,000 in 2024 to 7.7 in 2025, mostly among boys (WA DOH, via KOMO).
    • King County: in a University of Washington review, three-quarters of suicide deaths among children ages 10–14 involved firearms, usually a family member's gun (report coverage). Locked storage is the single most protective step at home. King County's LOK-IT-UP program offers discounted lock boxes.
    • County-level data: Public Health – Seattle & King County suicide data.

    If a student or family member is struggling: call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, 24/7). For teens, Crisis Connections' Teen Link is 866-833-6546.

    A psychologist's reading of HYS numbers. These are self-reports from the students who took part, and participation drops sharply by 12th grade. Differences of a few points between places are often within the margin of error. The protective factors are the most useful to track: having an adult to turn to, feeling safe at school, and hope. They respond to school climate in ways that suicide rates, which are rare events, can't show year to year.

    Safety policies and approaches

    State law requires every district to have certain policies, and districts differ most in how they staff security. Use this as a starting point and confirm current practice with each district.

    Required in every Washington district: a harassment, intimidation and bullying (HIB) policy with a named compliance officer (RCW 28A.600.477, model policy 3207). A behavioral threat-assessment program (RCW 28A.320.123). Discipline rules that limit exclusion and require re-engagement meetings (WAC 392-400). A ban on firearms and dangerous weapons on school grounds (RCW 9.41.280). If a district has school resource officers, it must meet RCW 28A.320.124's training and agreement rules.
